# Cedar Scholarship Foundation — investment committee decision

**Decision: Reject deployment for now. Commit 0% of new capital to the proposed strategy and leave Cedar's existing holdings in place.** Keep the model's allocation as a research candidate. This is my current recommendation, not an instruction to sell anything Cedar already owns.

The app found a mathematically feasible portfolio under its *testable* assumptions. I do not think that is enough to authorize an actual trade. My main concern is that the estimated return lands right on the screening hurdle, before Cedar's real transition costs are known. The biggest missing client decision is its approved volatility limit. Without that limit, calling a 3.53% estimate “low risk” does not tell the committee whether the risk is acceptable for Cedar's quarterly scholarship obligations. I would be more open to a small, committee-approved pilot if the risk limit is comfortably above the measured risk **and** the portfolio still clears an agreed after-cost hurdle under verified holdings and costs. This paragraph reflects my [recorded committee reflection](tuesday-studio-record.md#current-student-committee-reflection--november-19-2026).

## The result and what I make of it

Cedar's stated coursework objective is CPI plus 3% annually **after costs over rolling five-year periods**, while paying $150,000 in scholarships each quarter from a $20 million foundation. The app uses 60 monthly fund returns through September 30, 2026, for a frozen November 2, 2026, research decision. It adds an *illustrative* 2.50% CPI input to the 3.00% real spread to create a 5.50% nominal screening hurdle. That calculation is useful for comparing candidates; an arithmetic historical mean meeting 5.50% does not prove the five-year real, after-cost objective.

These are the [saved baseline results](../outputs/results.json), rounded to two decimals. The 60% VT / 40% LQD comparison portfolio estimates 6.81% return and 12.29% volatility, but has no BIL reserve; it is a comparison, not a Cedar-approved alternative.

The equal-weight and inverse-volatility portfolios miss the illustrative return hurdle. The constrained solver finds the lowest-variance mix that meets its hurdle and allocation rules. It puts most of the money in BIL, a short Treasury fund, and about 23% in VT, a global stock fund. That makes sense *within this model*: the [annualized historical return estimates](../outputs/results.json) are about 3.59% for BIL and 11.90% for VT, while IEF and LQD have negative estimates in this sample. The solver therefore uses enough VT to lift the mix to 5.50% and leaves IEF and LQD at zero. The zero weights do not prove those bond funds have no future value. They show what this particular estimation window and objective favored. As I explained in my [committee presentation](transcript-video-3.txt), the mix relies on VT to reach the hurdle; weaker future stock returns would change the case.

This is where I pushed back on the AI-assisted analysis: the candidate's 5.50% estimate looks reasonable beside 3.53% estimated volatility, but it has essentially no modeled return cushion. I asked what a “Pass” really means when the optimizer was instructed to reach 5.50% and landed exactly there. The app's default additional annual cost input is zero, and Cedar's starting holdings are unknown, so actual turnover and transition costs cannot be calculated. Even a small cost or a modest shortfall in expected return could put the candidate below the 5.50% screen. This is why I would not treat a solver “Pass” as an investment approval. My [recorded reflection](tuesday-studio-record.md#current-student-committee-reflection--november-19-2026) raised this concern before this memo rewrite.

## What I tested and what it changed

The ordinary 36-month estimation stress moved BIL from 77.04% to 92.86% and VT from 22.96% to 7.14%, a 15.82-percentage-point largest weight change. Its estimated return still reaches 5.50%, but the allocation is very different. This is evidence that the selected mix depends on the return sample. As I explained in my [committee presentation](transcript-video-3.txt), I would test whether the strategy remains acceptable across reasonable estimation windows rather than pick the answer I like best. The 50-basis-point trading-cost stress leaves the target weights essentially unchanged because it starts from hypothetical holdings equal to the target. A separate hypothetical trade incurs a 0.52% cost. Neither is Cedar's actual transition.

I also challenged the idea that a feasible baseline would remain feasible if the reserve assumption changed substantially. My [locked changed-input record](changed-input-record.md) shows the question and the test. Before the run, I predicted that raising the *analytical* BIL minimum from 3% to 85% would make 5.50% unattainable. With at most 15% left for VT, I estimated an 85% BIL / 15% VT mix would return about 4.84%. The saved run found no feasible constrained portfolio; it did not produce an “after” volatility figure. The recommendation stayed at 0% new capital. The 85% input was a stress scenario, **not** a change to Cedar's actual $600,000 reserve rule. It showed me that demanding a much larger short-term reserve can conflict with the return hurdle. It did not tell me that Cedar should adopt an 85% reserve.

I did not take the AI-proposed CPI fallback at face value. I [reported checking the source values and arithmetic](signal-data-card.md#student-reported-source-check--november-19-2026): the stored November 1 ALFRED vintage shows 336.298 for September 2026 and 324.245 for September 2025, and 336.298 / 324.245 − 1 is about 3.72%. That check supports the displayed number. I still question whether the rule belongs in an approved investment process. It raises the effective BIL floor from the entered 3% to 5%, but the baseline already holds 77.04% BIL, so this run gives no evidence that the rule improves the allocation. The 3.72% *historical* reading is also separate from the 2.50% *illustrative forward* CPI input used for the return hurdle. Checking arithmetic does not establish that Cedar approved the policy.

## Why approval is still missing

- **Risk limit:** The app estimates 3.53% annual volatility, but Cedar's approved numerical limit is absent. A low number relative to another portfolio may still be too high for a client funding quarterly scholarships. Volatility also does not describe every loss, liquidity, or spending risk. I would compare the candidate against Cedar's actual limit and examine adverse spending periods before authorizing it.
- **Reserve treatment:** The app treats BIL as an illustrative stand-in for the $600,000 cash/short-Treasury reserve. A fund holding short bills is not the same as a scheduled cash balance. Cedar must decide whether BIL counts and how scholarships will be paid when markets or fund prices move.
- **Current holdings and costs:** Without starting positions and a trading-cost schedule, the committee cannot know what must be sold, whether the 20% annual one-way turnover cap would be met, or whether the return clears the hurdle after implementation. A target portfolio is not an executable transition plan.
- **Return evidence and policy:** The 5.50% figure is a historical arithmetic estimate using an unconfirmed 2.50% CPI assumption and zero added annual costs. It cannot establish a rolling five-year real return after costs. Cedar also needs to review the proposed CPI signal policy. A changed assumption or weaker VT return estimate could change the portfolio and its margin over the hurdle.

The app marks testable constraints as passing for the candidate, but full mandate status is **Not Known** and deployment authorization **fails** because required client evidence is unresolved. Its separate holdout uses a later-downloaded adjusted-price snapshot, so I treat it as a diagnostic, not a point-in-time trading record. [Aidan's cold run](andrew-cold-run-by-aidan.md) reproduced the baseline decision and 5.50%/3.53% estimates from the README on macOS; he changed no inputs. That supports reproducibility of the earlier app version, not the investment case or every later edit.

## Committee action and reversal

I recommend **0% of new capital** until Cedar documents its risk limit, approves or rejects BIL as reserve coverage, provides actual holdings and cost information, and reviews the proposed CPI policy. Then I would rerun the app using the approved inputs, check actual transition turnover and after-cost results, and examine whether the candidate holds up across plausible return assumptions and estimation windows. If the candidate has room above the agreed hurdle and remains within Cedar's approved risk and spending constraints, I would bring a *small pilot* back for committee approval. If it does not, I would keep the allocation at 0% and revise the research strategy rather than quietly lower the objective.
